What is an Age Calculator?

An online age calculator is a convenient utility designed to compute your exact chronological age from your date of birth to the present day. Unlike simple mental subtraction that only considers years, this calculator accurately accounts for the varying number of days in each calendar month as well as leap years to give you your precise age in years, months, and days.

The primary inputs are your birth day, birth month, and birth year, which you can enter manually or select using the interactive calendar picker. The calculator also computes the exact time remaining until your upcoming birthday, the day of the week it will fall on, your astrological zodiac sign, and the total days and hours lived. This information is frequently required for school admissions, government job applications, insurance policies, competitive exams, and milestone celebrations.

How to Use the Age Calculator

  1. Enter your birth day (DD), month (MM), and year (YYYY) into the numeric input fields, or pick your birth date directly from the calendar picker.

  3. Instantly review your exact age displayed in complete years, months, and days.

  5. Click Reset at any time to clear the inputs and evaluate another date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this age calculator account for leap years?
Yes. The calculator recognizes leap years containing February 29 (including 1996, 2000, 2004, 2024, 2028, etc.) and precisely adjusts the total days lived and month-end transitions accordingly.
Is my personal birth date saved or transmitted online?
No. All date calculations run entirely within your web browser using client-side JavaScript. None of your entered personal details are transmitted to any server or stored in any database.
Can I calculate the age of a past event or business anniversary?
Yes. You can enter the starting date of any historical milestone, wedding anniversary, business founding, or vehicle registration to determine the exact elapsed duration.
Why does my age in days differ from multiplying years by 365?
Multiplying by 365 overlooks the extra day added during leap years and the differing lengths of individual months (28, 29, 30, or 31 days). Our tool counts every actual calendar day for complete accuracy.

What is an EMI Loan Calculator?

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) loan calculator is an essential personal finance tool that helps borrowers estimate their monthly repayment schedule before taking out credit. When you borrow money for a home purchase, car financing, higher education, or personal expenses, your loan must be repaid in equal monthly payments comprising both principal and interest components.

The primary inputs required are the loan principal amount, the annual interest rate offered by your bank, and the desired loan tenure in years or months. The calculator determines your fixed monthly EMI, the cumulative interest payable over the entire loan life, and the total repayment amount. Planning with an EMI calculator allows you to evaluate your monthly debt-to-income ratio, compare competitive offers from multiple lending institutions, and choose an affordable repayment horizon.

How to Use the EMI Calculator

  1. Enter your desired Loan Amount (Principal) in Indian Rupees (₹) or US Dollars ($).
  2. Input the bank's quoted Annual Interest Rate percentage (e.g., 8.5% p.a.).
  3. Specify the Loan Tenure by entering the duration and selecting years or months.
  4. Click Calculate EMI or adjust the values to see the monthly payment and total interest update in real time.
  5. Review the visual proportion bar to inspect the breakdown between your borrowed principal and the total interest charge.

Example Calculation

Explanation: Over 60 months, paying ₹20,517 every month completely clears the ₹10,00,000 principal plus ₹2,30,992 in accrued reducing-balance interest. The borrowed principal accounts for 81.2% of your total payments, while interest makes up 18.8%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula is used to calculate monthly EMI?
The calculator uses the standard reducing-balance formula: EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] ÷ [(1 + r)^n - 1], where P is principal, r is monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is total months.
How does choosing a longer loan tenure affect my EMI and total interest?
A longer tenure lowers your monthly installment, making immediate payments more manageable. However, because interest accrues over a longer period, your cumulative interest paid increases substantially.
Does this calculator include processing fees or loan insurance?
No. The calculator computes the pure mathematical amortization of principal and interest. Upfront bank processing fees, stamp duty, insurance premiums, and documentation charges are separate lender-specific costs.
Can prepayments reduce my future EMI or tenure?
Yes. Making partial prepayments directly reduces your outstanding principal, which enables your lender to either shorten your loan tenure or lower your subsequent monthly payments.

What is a Percentage Calculator?

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage increase manually?
Subtract the original value from the new value, divide that difference by the original value, and multiply by 100. If the result is positive, it represents an increase; if negative, a decrease.
Can a percentage be greater than 100%?
Yes. A value greater than 100% indicates that the quantity is larger than the base amount. For instance, 200% of 50 is 100, which represents double the original base.
What is the difference between percentage points and percentage change?
A percentage point is the simple arithmetic difference between two percentages (e.g., an interest rate rising from 5% to 7% is a 2 percentage point rise). However, the relative percentage change is a 40% increase [(7 - 5) ÷ 5 × 100].

What is a BMI Calculator?

A Body Mass Index (BMI) calculator is an internationally recognized anthropometric screening tool used to assess whether an individual's weight is appropriate for their height. Developed by the World Health Organization (WHO), BMI provides a standardized numerical indicator that categorizes individuals into Underweight, Normal weight, Overweight, or Obese categories.

The main inputs are your height and weight, which can be entered in either Metric units (centimeters and kilograms) or Imperial units (feet, inches, and pounds). The calculator computes your BMI score using the formula BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]² and displays your corresponding classification alongside your healthy weight range. While not a direct measure of body fat, BMI offers a helpful preliminary benchmark for evaluating weight-related wellness.

How to Use the BMI Calculator

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the official WHO BMI categories?
According to the World Health Organization: Underweight is below 18.5; Normal weight is 18.5 to 24.9; Overweight is 25.0 to 29.9; and Obesity is 30.0 or higher.
Does BMI distinguish between muscle mass and body fat?
No. BMI measures total body mass relative to height. Muscular athletes, bodybuilders, and heavy laborers may register in the overweight category despite having low body fat and excellent cardiovascular fitness.
Is this BMI calculator suitable for children and teenagers?
Standard adult BMI formulas are intended for individuals aged 18 and older. For children and adolescents aged 2 to 17, pediatricians utilize BMI-for-age growth percentiles rather than fixed adult categories.
Why does ethnicity impact healthy BMI thresholds?
Clinical research indicates that South Asian and East Asian populations often possess higher body fat percentages at lower BMI levels, leading health authorities in those regions to define overweight starting at 23.0 rather than 25.0.
Medical Disclaimer: Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple statistical screening metric for adult populations and is NOT a medical diagnosis. It does not measure body fat distribution, muscle mass, bone density, age, genetic factors, or overall physical health. What is a GST Calculator?

A Goods and Services Tax (GST) calculator is a specialized commercial finance tool created to compute indirect tax liabilities on products and services under the Indian taxation framework. Under GST, multiple central and state taxes were consolidated into a unified destination-based tax system with standardized rate slabs (typically 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%).

The primary inputs include the transaction amount in Indian Rupees (₹), the applicable GST rate percentage, and the calculation mode: GST Exclusive (adding tax to the base price) or GST Inclusive (extracting pre-tax value from a gross total). The calculator provides clear splits for Central GST (CGST), State GST (SGST), and Integrated GST (IGST). Business owners, freelancers, accountants, and retail shoppers rely on this utility to ensure accurate billing, avoid invoicing errors, and understand tax contributions.

How to Use the GST Calculator

  1. Select the calculation type: choose GST Exclusive (Add Tax) to add GST to a base figure, or GST Inclusive (Remove Tax) to extract tax from a gross price.
  2. Enter the base or invoice amount in Indian Rupees (₹).
  3. Select a standard tax slab button (5%, 12%, 18%, or 28%) or type a custom tax rate.
  4. Click Calculate GST to display the gross amount, net base price, and total tax amount.
  5. Inspect the detailed tax breakdown table to see the equal CGST and SGST splits or the full IGST amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between CGST, SGST, and IGST?
When goods or services are sold within the same state (intra-state), tax is shared equally between the Central Government (CGST) and State Government (SGST). When goods are sold across state borders (inter-state), Integrated GST (IGST) is levied and collected centrally.
What is the mathematical formula for GST inclusive calculation?
To remove GST from a gross total: Net Price = Gross Total ÷ [1 + (GST Rate ÷ 100)]. The GST tax amount is then calculated as Gross Total - Net Price.
Who needs to charge and collect GST?
Businesses and service providers whose annual aggregate turnover exceeds the statutory registration threshold (generally ₹40 lakhs for goods and ₹20 lakhs for services in India) are required to register for GST and collect tax on taxable supplies.
Can businesses claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on GST paid?
Yes. Registered taxpayers can typically offset the GST paid on business purchases and capital equipment (Input Tax Credit) against their output GST liability on sales.

What is a Simple Interest Calculator?

A simple interest calculator is a foundational financial utility that calculates the cost of borrowing money or the earnings on an investment where interest is determined strictly on the initial principal balance. Unlike compounding schemes where interest earns interest, simple interest remains constant across every payment interval throughout the agreed duration.

The primary inputs are the principal sum (the original amount borrowed or deposited), the annual interest rate percentage, and the time period specified in either years or months. The calculator determines the total accrued interest, the final maturity sum (Principal + Interest), and the annualized monthly and daily interest breakdowns. Simple interest calculations are standard in informal personal loans, short-term promissory notes, auto financing arrangements, and short-term agricultural credits.

How to Use the Simple Interest Calculator

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the mathematical formula for simple interest?
The formula is: SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100, where P is the principal amount, R is the annual interest rate, and T is time in years. If time is given in months, T equals Months ÷ 12.
How does simple interest differ from compound interest?
In simple interest, earnings are calculated solely on the original principal sum. In compound interest, accumulated interest from previous periods is added to the principal base, allowing your balance to grow exponentially.
When is simple interest typically utilized?
Simple interest is widely used for short-term personal lending, educational loans during study moratoriums, pawn transactions, and debt instruments with non-compounding covenants.
Does simple interest change if interest is paid out monthly?
No. Because interest is not reinvested into the principal, the periodic interest payout remains identical every month for the entire duration of the loan.

What is a Compound Interest Calculator?

A compound interest calculator is an advanced wealth-planning tool that projects the exponential growth of savings and investments over time. Often described as "interest on interest," compounding occurs when earned interest is added back into the principal balance, so that subsequent interest is calculated on a progressively larger base.

The Present Value Eligibility Formula

First, the maximum permissible new monthly EMI is determined:

Max New EMI = (Net Monthly Salary × FOIR%) − Existing EMIs

Next, the maximum principal is calculated using the standard discounted present value formula for an ordinary annuity:

Max Loan Amount = [Max EMI × ((1 + r)^n − 1)] / [r × (1 + r)^n]

Where r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100) and n is the total tenure in months.

Most borrowers are accustomed to choosing a loan tenure and accepting whatever EMI the bank assigns. Mathematical Formula for Loan Tenure

Using the logarithmic amortization formula derived from compound annuity math:

n = − ln(1 − (P × r / EMI)) / ln(1 + r)

Where:

  • n = Total number of monthly installments required.
  • P = Loan principal amount.
  • r = Periodic monthly interest rate = (Annual Rate / 12 / 100).
  • EMI = Chosen monthly payment amount.
  • ln = Natural logarithm function.

Critical Condition: The chosen monthly payment must be strictly greater than P × r (the first month's interest). If it is less, the loan balance will grow indefinitely through negative amortization.

What Is Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) and Why It Matters

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) represents the minimum amount of energy (calories) required to maintain vital physiological life functions while your body is at complete physical, digestive, and emotional rest. Even when you are sleeping in a climate-controlled room, your heart beats, lungs respire, brain fires neurons, and cells undergo continuous protein synthesis. BMR typically accounts for 60% to 75% of your total daily energy expenditure.

Factors That Influence Your BMR

  • Lean Muscle Mass: Muscle tissue burns approximately 3 times more calories at rest per kilogram than adipose fat tissue. Resistance training increases your baseline BMR.
  • Age: Metabolic rate declines by roughly 1-2% per decade after age 30, primarily due to gradual loss of muscle mass (sarcopenia).
  • Thyroid Function: Thyroid hormones (T3 and T4) act as the master thermostat of metabolic rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I ever eat fewer calories than my BMR?
Eating below your BMR is generally not advised unless supervised by a physician. Consuming fewer calories than your resting organs need can prompt metabolic adaptation, muscle wasting, and hormone dysregulation.
How does BMR differ from RMR?
BMR requires measurement after a strict 12-hour fast in a specialized thermal chamber. Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR) is measured under less restrictive resting conditions and is typically 5-10% higher than BMR.
Can drinking cold water boost my BMR?
Drinking cold water requires your body to expend a negligible number of calories to warm the water to body temperature, but the effect is too small (approx. 8 kcal per glass) to meaningfully alter daily BMR.
Does fasting slow down BMR?
Short-term intermittent fasting (16-24 hours) does not reduce BMR. Prolonged severe starvation or chronic extreme caloric restriction over multiple weeks, however, triggers adaptive thermogenesis.

Mastering Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

While BMR tells you what you burn while resting, Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) tells you the true total number of calories you burn across an entire 24-hour day of living, working, digesting, and exercising. Knowing your exact TDEE removes the guesswork from bodybuilding cutting phases, endurance prep, and sustainable weight management.

The 4 Pillars of Daily Calorie Burn

  • Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR ~60-70%): Energy burned by internal organs at complete rest.
  • Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT ~15%): Unplanned movement: walking to work, fidgeting, carrying groceries, climbing stairs.
  • Thermic Effect of Food (TEF ~10%): Energy used to digest and process proteins, carbohydrates, and fats.
  • Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (EAT ~5-15%): Planned physical workouts, sports, weightlifting, and cardio sessions.

Cutting target = 2,527 − 500 = 2,027 kcal/day.

Age: 35 | Weight: 85 kg | Height: 180 cm | Sedentary (1.2x).

Tips for Elevating Your TDEE

  • Boost your NEAT: Take telephone calls while pacing, use a standing desk, or aim for 8,000-10,000 steps daily. NEAT often burns more calories than a 45-minute gym session.
  • Increase dietary protein: Protein has a high thermic effect (20-30% of its calories are burned during digestion, compared to only 5-10% for carbs and 0-3% for fats).
  • Build muscle mass: Increasing functional lean mass permanently raises baseline daily caloric demands.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does TDEE differ from BMR?
BMR is the calories burned doing nothing in bed. TDEE is BMR plus the extra calories burned by eating, standing, walking, working, and working out.
What should I do if my weight does not change after 2 weeks at target TDEE?
TDEE formulas provide mathematical estimates. If your weight is stable over 14 days, your actual intake is your true maintenance level. Adjust by 150-200 kcal up or down to continue progress.
Does TDEE fluctuate daily?
Yes. Days with intense training or long walks burn significantly more calories than rest days spent sitting. Many athletes cycle calories between training days and rest days.
Can drinking coffee increase TDEE?
Caffeine provides a mild temporary thermogenic boost of roughly 3-11% for 1-2 hours, but this does not replace consistent dietary habit adherence.

How Pregnancy Due Dates and Gestational Age Are Calculated

A full-term human pregnancy lasts an average of 280 days (40 weeks) counted from the first day of the mother's last menstrual period (LMP). Your due date serves as a vital clinical guidepost for scheduling developmental prenatal ultrasounds, maternal blood screenings, and labor preparations.

The Medical Formula: Naegele's Rule

For a standard 28-day cycle, Naegele's rule calculates the EDD as:

Due Date = First Day of LMP + 1 Year − 3 Months + 7 Days

Cycle Adjustment: If your cycle is C days (where C ≠ 28), add (C − 28) days to the result. For example, in a 32-day cycle, ovulation occurs roughly 4 days later, so 4 days are added to the estimated due date.

The Three Pregnancy Trimesters

  • First Trimester (Weeks 1 to 13): Major organogenesis, heartbeat detection, rapid cellular differentiation, and initial dating scan (nuchal translucency).
  • Second Trimester (Weeks 14 to 27): "The golden trimester": fetal movements ("quickening") begin, anatomy ultrasound (anomaly scan) at weeks 18-20, and viability reached at week 24.
  • Third Trimester (Weeks 28 to 40+): Rapid fetal weight gain, lung maturation, descent into the pelvis, and preparation for labor. Full term is achieved at 37 weeks.

Tips for Expecting Parents

  • Only 4% of babies arrive on their exact due date: Approximately 90% of healthy infants are born within a two-week window either side of their EDD (between 38 and 42 weeks).
  • First trimester ultrasound is most accurate: A crown-rump length (CRL) ultrasound scan performed between weeks 8 and 13 has an error margin of only ±5 days and is considered the clinical gold standard.
  • Start folic acid early: Taking 400 mcg of folic acid daily reduces neural tube defect risks significantly.

Thermodynamic Scales: Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin

Temperature quantifies the average kinetic energy of vibrating atoms and molecules in a substance. Unlike length or weight where zero signifies nothingness, zero on the Celsius (°C) scale represents the freezing point of pure water at one standard atmosphere, while zero on the Fahrenheit (°F) scale represents the freezing point of a saltwater brine.   • Freezing Point of Water: 0 °C = 32 °F = 273.15 K
  • Comfortable Room Temperature: 20 to 22 °C = 68 to 72 °F
  • Boiling Point of Water: 100 °C = 212 °F = 373.15 K
  • The Coincidence Point: −40 °C is exactly equal to −40 °F!

Kelvin is an absolute thermodynamic unit of measurement rather than an arbitrary scale with an offset zero point. Therefore, standard scientific convention writes 300 K rather than 300 °K.

At exactly −40°. When it is −40 °C outside, it is also −40 °F.

No. 0 Kelvin is Absolute Zero, the thermodynamic limit where subatomic particles possess minimum quantum zero-point vibrational energy.

The Truth About Stacked Discounts: Why 30% + 10% Does NOT Equal 40%

Retail stores and e-commerce shopping portals often advertise promotions with compounding promotions: "30% off storewide PLUS take an additional 10% off at checkout with code SAVE10!" Many shoppers mistakenly assume they are getting 40% off the original price. However, successive discounts apply sequentially: the second discount is applied only to the already reduced price, resulting in an effective 37% discount rather than 40%.

How to Use This Discount + Final Price Calculator

  1. Enter the original Tag Price of the merchandise.
  2. Enter the Primary Discount Percentage (e.g. 30%).
  3. Enter any Additional Stacked Discount (e.g. 10% coupon code or loyalty rebate).
  4. Specify the Sales Tax / GST percentage to be added at checkout (or leave as 0% if prices are tax-inclusive).
  5. Click Calculate Final Price to see the exact checkout price, total rupee or dollar savings, and the true effective discount rate.

Mathematical Formula for Successive Discounts

Price 1 = Original Price × (1 − (Discount 1 / 100))

Price 2 = Price 1 × (1 − (Discount 2 / 100))

Effective Combined Discount (%) = 100 − [(100 − D1) × (100 − D2) / 100]

Tax Amount = Price 2 × (Tax Rate / 100)

Final Price = Price 2 + Tax Amount

Practical Examples

Example 1: Designer Jacket Sale (INR ₹)
Tag Price: ₹2,500 | Store Sale: 30% off | App Coupon: Extra 10% off | GST: 12%.
Step 1: ₹2,500 − 30% (₹750) = ₹1,750.
Step 2: ₹1,750 − 10% (₹175) = ₹1,575.
Effective Discount = 37% off (saving ₹925 before tax).
Step 3: Add 12% GST (+₹189) = ₹1,764.00 Final Checkout Price.
Example 2: 50% + 50% Trap
Original: $100. First 50% drops it to $50. Second 50% drops it to $25.
Final price is $25 (75% off), NOT free ($0)!

Tips for Smart Shopping

  • Evaluate the true effective discount: Whenever you see "take an extra X% off already discounted items", calculate the true net percentage before purchasing.
  • Watch for tax on discounted vs original price: By law in most jurisdictions, sales tax / GST is assessed on the final discounted sale price, not the original MSRP.
  • Compare minimum threshold coupons: If a coupon requires a ₹2,000 cart minimum to save ₹200, purchasing ₹400 of unwanted goods just to unlock the coupon actually costs you more.

Understanding Sales Tax: Exclusive vs Inclusive Calculations

Sales tax is a consumption tax charged on the sale of retail goods and services. Depending on the nation and local jurisdiction, sales tax may be either exclusive (added to the price tag at the register, standard across the United States and Canada) or inclusive (embedded within the displayed shelf price, standard for Value Added Tax [VAT] and GST in Europe, Australia, and India). Knowing how to calculate both forward and in reverse is indispensable for accounting, invoicing, and auditing.

How to Use This Sales Tax Calculator

  1. Select your currency (₹ INR or $ USD).
  2. Choose your calculation mode: Add Tax (if starting with pre-tax price) or Remove Tax (if starting with the total checkout price).
  3. Enter the dollar or rupee amount.
  4. Enter the local sales tax rate percentage (e.g. 5%, 8.25%, 18%).
  5. Click Calculate Sales Tax to view the pre-tax base, exact tax dollar amount, and total gross cost.

The Sales Tax Formulas

1. Forward Calculation (Adding Sales Tax):

Tax Amount = Net Pre-Tax Amount × (Tax Rate / 100)

Gross Total = Net Pre-Tax Amount + Tax Amount

2. Reverse Calculation (Extracting Embedded Sales Tax):

Net Pre-Tax Amount = Gross Total / (1 + (Tax Rate / 100))

Tax Amount = Gross Total − Net Pre-Tax Amount

Practical Examples

Example 1: US Retail Purchase (Adding Tax)
Shelf Price: $500.00 | Local Sales Tax Rate: 8.25% (e.g. California / Texas).
Tax Amount = $500.00 × 0.0825 = $41.25.
Final Checkout Total = $541.25.
Example 2: Reverse Tax Extraction (Indian Consulting Invoice)
Total Receipt Amount: ₹11,800 | GST Rate: 18%.
Pre-Tax Base = ₹11,800 / (1 + 0.18) = ₹11,800 / 1.18 = ₹10,000.00.
Embedded Sales Tax = ₹11,800 − ₹10,000 = ₹1,800.00.

Tips for Businesses and Consumers

  • Never calculate reverse tax with simple subtraction: Subtracting 18% from ₹11,800 gives ₹9,676 (wrong!). You must divide by 1.18 to determine the true pre-tax base of ₹10,000.
  • Tax exemptions and holidays: Many US states offer annual sales tax holidays on school supplies, apparel, and energy-efficient appliances where tax rates drop to 0%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Sales Tax and VAT / GST?
Sales tax is a single-stage tax levied only at the final point of sale to the end consumer. Value Added Tax (VAT) and Goods & Services Tax (GST) are multi-stage taxes collected at each step of the supply chain with input tax credits.
Why does sales tax vary so much between cities in the United States?
In the US, there is no federal sales tax. Instead, individual states, counties, and municipal cities set their own combined local sales taxes, ranging from 0% (Delaware, Oregon, Montana, New Hampshire) to over 10% (parts of California, Louisiana, Tennessee).
How do I back-calculate the tax rate if I only have pre-tax and post-tax prices?
Divide the tax amount by the pre-tax amount and multiply by 100: Tax Rate % = (Tax Amount / Pre-Tax Amount) × 100.
Are online e-commerce purchases subject to sales tax?
Since the US Supreme Court ruling in South Dakota v. Wayfair (2018), online retailers are required to collect state and local sales tax based on the delivery destination address of the customer.
