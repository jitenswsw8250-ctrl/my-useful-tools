About Goods and Services Tax (GST)

Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based indirect tax levied on the manufacture, sale, and consumption of goods and services throughout India.

GST Exclusive vs. GST Inclusive

  • GST Exclusive: The entered amount is the pre-tax base cost. GST is added on top to reach the final invoice total.
    Formula: GST Amount = (Net Price × GST Rate) ÷ 100 | Total Price = Net Price + GST Amount
  • GST Inclusive: The entered amount already includes tax. This calculator extracts the underlying net price and the exact tax paid.
    Formula: GST Amount = Total Price - [Total Price ÷ (1 + (GST Rate ÷ 100))] | Net Price = Total Price - GST Amount

Tax Components: CGST, SGST, and IGST

Under the dual GST model in India, every intra-state supply is subject to Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST) in equal halves. Inter-state supplies are taxed under Integrated GST (IGST), collected by the Central Government.

What is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is a quick, straightforward method of calculating the interest charge on a loan or the earnings from an investment where interest is determined solely on the original principal amount.

The Simple Interest Formula

SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100

  • P: Principal sum borrowed or invested
  • R: Annual interest rate percentage
  • T: Time duration in years (if given in months, T = Months ÷ 12)
  • Maturity Amount (A): A = P + SI

Simple interest is commonly used in short-term personal lending, informal promissory notes, and certain auto loan contracts.

Understanding Compound Interest

Compound interest is often described as "interest on interest." Unlike simple interest, where earnings remain fixed each cycle, compound interest adds earned interest back into the principal sum so that future interest is computed on an ever-growing base.

The Compound Interest Formula

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t)

  • A: Final maturity amount
  • P: Initial principal balance
  • r: Annual nominal interest rate (in decimal, e.g. 8% = 0.08)
  • n: Number of compounding periods per year (e.g. 4 for quarterly)
  • t: Total investment duration in years
  • Compound Interest (CI): CI = A - P

More frequent compounding (such as monthly or quarterly compared to annually) generates higher effective returns due to earlier reinvestment of interest.

Profit & Loss Concepts & Formulas

In trade and commercial business, understanding whether a transaction yields a profit or loss—and calculating the exact percentage—is vital for pricing products accurately and sustaining business health.

Core Definitions

  • Cost Price (CP): The actual purchase price or manufacturing cost of an item plus associated expenses (overheads, shipping, packaging).
  • Selling Price (SP): The price at which the good or service is sold to the customer.

Formulas

  • If SP > Total Cost: Profit = SP - Total Cost | Profit % = (Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100
  • If SP < Total Cost: Loss = Total Cost - SP | Loss % = (Loss ÷ Total Cost) × 100
  • Profit Margin: Margin % = (Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100
  • Markup: Markup % = (Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100

What is a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows you to invest a fixed amount regularly (typically every month) into mutual funds. It fosters financial discipline and averages out market volatility through Rupee Cost Averaging.

The SIP Future Value Formula

M = P × [((1 + i)^n - 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)

  • M: Estimated future maturity value
  • P: Monthly investment amount
  • i: Periodic monthly rate of return = (Annual Rate ÷ 12) ÷ 100
  • n: Total number of monthly installments = Years × 12

By investing regularly over long periods, compounding allows investment returns to generate their own returns, helping you achieve major life goals like retirement, home purchase, or education funds.

About Fixed Deposits (FDs)

A Fixed Deposit is a financial instrument provided by banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) where depositors lock in an amount for a predetermined duration at a guaranteed interest rate.

How Bank FD Interest is Calculated

In India and many banking jurisdictions, interest on cumulative fixed deposits with tenures exceeding 6 months is compounded quarterly (4 times a year). The maturity value is computed using the compound interest formula:

A = P × (1 + r ÷ 4)^(4 × t)

Senior citizens generally receive a higher interest rate (typically 0.50% p.a. additional) across major public and private sector banks.

Sexagesimal Time Calculation & Decimal Hours

Time mathematics operates on a base-60 (sexagesimal) system where 1 hour = 60 minutes and 1 minute = 60 seconds.

Converting Minutes to Decimal Hours for Payroll

Payroll and client timesheets require hours formatted in decimal form rather than minutes. To convert, divide minutes by 60:

Decimal Hours = Hours + (Minutes ÷ 60) + (Seconds ÷ 3600)

Example: 8 hours and 30 minutes = 8 + (30 ÷ 60) = 8.50 hours.

Mean, Median, and Mode: How to Use Each

In statistics, the "average" can refer to multiple measures of central tendency, each serving a unique diagnostic purpose.

1. Arithmetic Mean

The sum of all values divided by the total count of values: Mean = (x₁ + x₂ + ... + xₙ) ÷ n. While highly useful, the mean is susceptible to distortion by extreme outliers.

2. Median

The exact middle value when data is sorted in ascending order. If the count is even, the median is the average of the two middle numbers. The median is far more resilient to skewed distributions and outliers (e.g. median household income or home prices).

3. Mode

The value or values that appear most frequently in a dataset. A dataset may be unimodal, bimodal, multimodal, or have no mode if all values appear an equal number of times.
