What is an Age Calculator?

An online age calculator is a convenient utility designed to compute your exact chronological age from your date of birth to the present day. Unlike simple mental subtraction that only considers years, this calculator accurately accounts for the varying number of days in each calendar month as well as leap years to give you your precise age in years, months, and days.

The primary inputs are your birth day, birth month, and birth year, which you can enter manually or select using the interactive calendar picker. The calculator also computes the exact time remaining until your upcoming birthday, the day of the week it will fall on, your astrological zodiac sign, and the total days and hours lived. This information is frequently required for school admissions, government job applications, insurance policies, competitive exams, and milestone celebrations.

How to Use the Age Calculator

  1. Enter your birth day (DD), month (MM), and year (YYYY) into the numeric input fields, or pick your birth date directly from the calendar picker.
  2. Click the Calculate Age button to process your date.
  3. Instantly review your exact age displayed in complete years, months, and days.
  4. Check the breakdown cards below to view the countdown to your next birthday and total days lived.
  5. Click Reset at any time to clear the inputs and evaluate another date.

Example Calculation

Birth Date: June 15, 1995 | Reference Date: September 22, 2026

Result: 31 Years, 3 Months, 7 Days

Explanation: Between June 15, 1995 and June 15, 2026 is exactly 31 completed years. From June 15 to September 15 is 3 full months, plus 7 additional calendar days to reach September 22. The calculator also identifies your next birthday on Tuesday, June 15, 2027.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this age calculator account for leap years?
Yes. The calculator recognizes leap years containing February 29 (including 1996, 2000, 2004, 2024, 2028, etc.) and precisely adjusts the total days lived and month-end transitions accordingly.
Is my personal birth date saved or transmitted online?
No. All date calculations run entirely within your web browser using client-side JavaScript. None of your entered personal details are transmitted to any server or stored in any database.
Can I calculate the age of a past event or business anniversary?
Yes. You can enter the starting date of any historical milestone, wedding anniversary, business founding, or vehicle registration to determine the exact elapsed duration.
Why does my age in days differ from multiplying years by 365?
Multiplying by 365 overlooks the extra day added during leap years and the differing lengths of individual months (28, 29, 30, or 31 days). Our tool counts every actual calendar day for complete accuracy.

What is an EMI Loan Calculator?

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) loan calculator is an essential personal finance tool that helps borrowers estimate their monthly repayment schedule before taking out credit. When you borrow money for a home purchase, car financing, higher education, or personal expenses, your loan must be repaid in equal monthly payments comprising both principal and interest components.

The primary inputs required are the loan principal amount, the annual interest rate offered by your bank, and the desired loan tenure in years or months. The calculator determines your fixed monthly EMI, the cumulative interest payable over the entire loan life, and the total repayment amount. Planning with an EMI calculator allows you to evaluate your monthly debt-to-income ratio, compare competitive offers from multiple lending institutions, and choose an affordable repayment horizon.

How to Use the EMI Calculator

  1. Enter your desired Loan Amount (Principal) in Indian Rupees (₹) or US Dollars ($).
  2. Input the bank's quoted Annual Interest Rate percentage (e.g., 8.5% p.a.).
  3. Specify the Loan Tenure by entering the duration and selecting years or months.
  4. Click Calculate EMI or adjust the values to see the monthly payment and total interest update in real time.
  5. Review the visual proportion bar to inspect the breakdown between your borrowed principal and the total interest charge.

Example Calculation

Loan Amount: ₹10,00,000 | Interest Rate: 8.5% per annum | Tenure: 5 Years (60 Months)

Monthly EMI: ₹20,517 | Total Interest: ₹2,30,992 | Total Repayment: ₹12,30,992

Explanation: Over 60 months, paying ₹20,517 every month completely clears the ₹10,00,000 principal plus ₹2,30,992 in accrued reducing-balance interest. The borrowed principal accounts for 81.2% of your total payments, while interest makes up 18.8%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula is used to calculate monthly EMI?
The calculator uses the standard reducing-balance formula: EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] ÷ [(1 + r)^n - 1], where P is principal, r is monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is total months.
How does choosing a longer loan tenure affect my EMI and total interest?
A longer tenure lowers your monthly installment, making immediate payments more manageable. However, because interest accrues over a longer period, your cumulative interest paid increases substantially.
Does this calculator include processing fees or loan insurance?
No. The calculator computes the pure mathematical amortization of principal and interest. Upfront bank processing fees, stamp duty, insurance premiums, and documentation charges are separate lender-specific costs.
Can prepayments reduce my future EMI or tenure?
Yes. Making partial prepayments directly reduces your outstanding principal, which enables your lender to either shorten your loan tenure or lower your subsequent monthly payments.
Financial Disclaimer: Calculations provided by this tool are informational estimates based on standard amortization formulas. Actual lender loan terms, interest compounding methods, processing fees, GST, and final EMI amounts may vary based on your lender's policies and credit assessment.

What is a Percentage Calculator?

A percentage calculator is an interactive math utility designed to solve common everyday percentage problems quickly and accurately. Percentages express numbers as fractions of 100, providing an intuitive way to understand proportions, financial markups, discount savings, test scores, tip allocations, and economic indicators.

This tool offers three distinct operational modes: calculating a direct percentage of any number (e.g., finding 15% of 250), determining the percentage increase or decrease between two values (e.g., measuring price inflation or revenue growth), and finding what percentage one number represents of another (e.g., scoring 45 out of 60 on an exam). Utilizing this calculator eliminates guesswork and mental math errors in business accounting, academic coursework, and daily shopping.

How to Use the Percentage Calculator

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a percentage increase manually?
Subtract the original value from the new value, divide that difference by the original value, and multiply by 100. If the result is positive, it represents an increase; if negative, a decrease.
Can a percentage be greater than 100%?
Yes. A value greater than 100% indicates that the quantity is larger than the base amount. For instance, 200% of 50 is 100, which represents double the original base.
What is the difference between percentage points and percentage change?
A percentage point is the simple arithmetic difference between two percentages (e.g., an interest rate rising from 5% to 7% is a 2 percentage point rise). However, the relative percentage change is a 40% increase [(7 - 5) ÷ 5 × 100].

What is a BMI Calculator?

A Body Mass Index (BMI) calculator is an internationally recognized anthropometric screening tool used to assess whether an individual's weight is appropriate for their height. Developed by the World Health Organization (WHO), BMI provides a standardized numerical indicator that categorizes individuals into Underweight, Normal weight, Overweight, or Obese categories.

The main inputs are your height and weight, which can be entered in either Metric units (centimeters and kilograms) or Imperial units (feet, inches, and pounds). The calculator computes your BMI score using the formula BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]² and displays your corresponding classification alongside your healthy weight range. While not a direct measure of body fat, BMI offers a helpful preliminary benchmark for evaluating weight-related wellness.

How to Use the BMI Calculator

  1. Choose your preferred measurement system by selecting the Metric (cm / kg) or Imperial (ft, in / lbs) tab.
  2. Enter your height and weight accurately into the respective input fields.
  3. Click the Calculate BMI button to display your results.
  4. Observe your score, the visual WHO classification indicator, and your recommended healthy weight range.
  5. Click Reset to return the calculator to default baseline values.

Explanation: With a height of 1.75 meters, height squared equals 3.0625. Dividing 70 kg by 3.0625 yields a BMI of 22.86, placing the individual comfortably within the healthy WHO standard range of 18.5 to 24.9.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the official WHO BMI categories?
According to the World Health Organization: Underweight is below 18.5; Normal weight is 18.5 to 24.9; Overweight is 25.0 to 29.9; and Obesity is 30.0 or higher.
Does BMI distinguish between muscle mass and body fat?
No. BMI measures total body mass relative to height. Muscular athletes, bodybuilders, and heavy laborers may register in the overweight category despite having low body fat and excellent cardiovascular fitness.
Is this BMI calculator suitable for children and teenagers?
Standard adult BMI formulas are intended for individuals aged 18 and older. For children and adolescents aged 2 to 17, pediatricians utilize BMI-for-age growth percentiles rather than fixed adult categories.
Why does ethnicity impact healthy BMI thresholds?
Clinical research indicates that South Asian and East Asian populations often possess higher body fat percentages at lower BMI levels, leading health authorities in those regions to define overweight starting at 23.0 rather than 25.0.
Medical Disclaimer: Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple statistical screening metric for adult populations and is NOT a medical diagnosis. It does not measure body fat distribution, muscle mass, bone density, age, genetic factors, or overall physical health. Please consult a licensed physician or registered healthcare provider for personalized medical evaluation and dietary advice.

What is a GST Calculator?

A Goods and Services Tax (GST) calculator is a specialized commercial finance tool created to compute indirect tax liabilities on products and services under the Indian taxation framework. Under GST, multiple central and state taxes were consolidated into a unified destination-based tax system with standardized rate slabs (typically 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%).

The primary inputs include the transaction amount in Indian Rupees (₹), the applicable GST rate percentage, and the calculation mode: GST Exclusive (adding tax to the base price) or GST Inclusive (extracting pre-tax value from a gross total). The calculator provides clear splits for Central GST (CGST), State GST (SGST), and Integrated GST (IGST). Business owners, freelancers, accountants, and retail shoppers rely on this utility to ensure accurate billing, avoid invoicing errors, and understand tax contributions.

How to Use the GST Calculator

  1. Select the calculation type: choose GST Exclusive (Add Tax) to add GST to a base figure, or GST Inclusive (Remove Tax) to extract tax from a gross price.
  2. Enter the base or invoice amount in Indian Rupees (₹).
  3. Select a standard tax slab button (5%, 12%, 18%, or 28%) or type a custom tax rate.
  4. Click Calculate GST to display the gross amount, net base price, and total tax amount.
  5. Inspect the detailed tax breakdown table to see the equal CGST and SGST splits or the full IGST amount.

Example Calculation

Base Amount: ₹10,00,000 | GST Rate: 18% | Mode: GST Exclusive

Net Price: ₹10,000 | Total GST: ₹1,800 | Gross Total: ₹11,800

Tax Split: CGST: ₹900 (9%) + SGST: ₹900 (9%) for intra-state sales, or IGST: ₹1,800 (18%) for inter-state transactions.

Inclusive Mode Comparison: If an invoice total is ₹11,800 inclusive of 18% GST, the underlying net base price is ₹10,000 (11,800 ÷ 1.18) and the tax paid is ₹1,800.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between CGST, SGST, and IGST?
When goods or services are sold within the same state (intra-state), tax is shared equally between the Central Government (CGST) and State Government (SGST). When goods are sold across state borders (inter-state), Integrated GST (IGST) is levied and collected centrally.
What is the mathematical formula for GST inclusive calculation?
To remove GST from a gross total: Net Price = Gross Total ÷ [1 + (GST Rate ÷ 100)]. The GST tax amount is then calculated as Gross Total - Net Price.
Who needs to charge and collect GST?
Businesses and service providers whose annual aggregate turnover exceeds the statutory registration threshold (generally ₹40 lakhs for goods and ₹20 lakhs for services in India) are required to register for GST and collect tax on taxable supplies.
Can businesses claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on GST paid?
Yes. Registered taxpayers can typically offset the GST paid on business purchases and capital equipment (Input Tax Credit) against their output GST liability on sales.
Taxation Disclaimer: This calculator provides general computational estimates for commercial convenience. Actual tax assessments depend on current Goods and Services Tax notifications, HSN/SAC classifications, reverse charge mechanisms, and statutory filing guidelines.

What is a Simple Interest Calculator?

A simple interest calculator is a foundational financial utility that calculates the cost of borrowing money or the earnings on an investment where interest is determined strictly on the initial principal balance. Unlike compounding schemes where interest earns interest, simple interest remains constant across every payment interval throughout the agreed duration.

The primary inputs are the principal sum (the original amount borrowed or deposited), the annual interest rate percentage, and the time period specified in either years or months. The calculator determines the total accrued interest, the final maturity sum (Principal + Interest), and the annualized monthly and daily interest breakdowns. Simple interest calculations are standard in informal personal loans, short-term promissory notes, auto financing arrangements, and short-term agricultural credits.

How to Use the Simple Interest Calculator

  1. Enter the original Principal Amount in Indian Rupees (₹).
  2. Input the agreed Annual Interest Rate percentage per annum (% p.a.).
  3. Specify the Time Period and select whether the duration is in Years or Months.

  5. Inspect the visual proportion bar to compare the principal versus interest ratio, or click Reset to start over.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the mathematical formula for simple interest?
The formula is: SI = (P × R × T) ÷ 100, where P is the principal amount, R is the annual interest rate, and T is time in years. If time is given in months, T equals Months ÷ 12.
How does simple interest differ from compound interest?
In simple interest, earnings are calculated solely on the original principal sum. In compound interest, accumulated interest from previous periods is added to the principal base, allowing your balance to grow exponentially.
When is simple interest typically utilized?
Simple interest is widely used for short-term personal lending, educational loans during study moratoriums, pawn transactions, and debt instruments with non-compounding covenants.
Does simple interest change if interest is paid out monthly?
No. Because interest is not reinvested into the principal, the periodic interest payout remains identical every month for the entire duration of the loan.

What is a Compound Interest Calculator?

A compound interest calculator is an advanced wealth-planning tool that projects the exponential growth of savings and investments over time. Often described as "interest on interest," compounding occurs when earned interest is added back into the principal balance, so that subsequent interest is calculated on a progressively larger base.

The primary inputs include the principal deposit in Indian Rupees (₹), the annual nominal interest rate, the investment duration in years, and the compounding frequency (annually, semi-annually, quarterly, or monthly). The calculator computes your future maturity balance, cumulative compound interest, the Effective Annual Rate (EAR / APY), and the exact extra earnings gained compared to simple interest. This tool is indispensable for evaluating mutual funds, bonds, long-term bank deposits, and retirement portfolios.

How to Use the Compound Interest Calculator

  1. Enter your starting Principal Amount in Indian Rupees (₹).
  2. Input the expected Annual Interest Rate percentage (% p.a.).
  3. Enter your planned Time Period in years.
  4. Select the Compounding Frequency from the dropdown (Annually, Half-yearly, Quarterly, or Monthly).
  5. Click Calculate Compound Interest to review your projected maturity balance, earned interest, and effective annual rate.

Comparison: Simple interest would have generated ₹40,000. Quarterly compounding generates ₹8,595 in extra wealth due to compounding. The Effective Annual Rate is 8.24%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula is used to calculate compound interest?
The compound interest future value formula is: A = P × [1 + (r ÷ n)]^(n × t), where P is principal, r is nominal annual rate as a decimal, n is compounding cycles per year, and t is time in years.
How does compounding frequency impact returns?
The more frequently interest compounds (such as monthly or quarterly compared to annually), the faster your balance grows, resulting in a higher Effective Annual Rate (EAR).
What is the "Rule of 72" in compound interest?
The Rule of 72 is a quick estimation shortcut: divide 72 by your annual interest rate to find roughly how many years it will take to double your investment. At 8% annual return, an investment doubles in approximately 9 years (72 ÷ 8 = 9).
Are compounding returns affected by inflation and taxes?
Yes. Real purchasing power depends on the inflation rate, and taxable investment earnings will be subject to applicable income tax or capital gains rates.
Investment Disclaimer: Projected compounding values are mathematical estimates based on constant assumed interest rates. Real-world financial market returns, dividend yields, taxation on interest, and inflation will affect your actual net wealth accumulation.

What is a Profit and Loss Calculator?

A profit and loss calculator is an essential commercial analysis utility designed for merchants, retail shopkeepers, ecommerce entrepreneurs, and wholesalers. It determines the financial success of commercial transactions by comparing the total cost of procuring or producing an item against its final selling price.

The primary inputs include the Cost Price (CP), the Selling Price (SP), and optional overhead expenses (such as shipping, packaging, warehousing, or labor). The calculator computes the exact monetary profit or loss, the percentage gain or loss on cost, the profit margin on selling price, and the markup percentage. Understanding these metrics prevents underpricing, protects cash flow, and ensures commercial sustainability.

How to Use the Profit & Loss Calculator

  1. Enter the item's purchase or manufacturing Cost Price (CP) in Indian Rupees (₹).
  2. Enter the expected or achieved Selling Price (SP).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Profit Margin and Markup?
Markup is the percentage added to the cost price to determine selling price: Markup = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100. Profit margin is the percentage of the selling price that is profit: Margin = (Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100. Margin is always lower than markup.
How does overhead cost affect my break-even point?
Overhead expenses (rent, marketing, transit) increase your total cost basis. Your break-even selling price equals your cost price plus all associated overheads; selling below that combined total results in a net operational loss.

Yes. Simply enter your direct hourly labor or contractor cost as the cost price, administrative overheads into the overhead field, and your client billing rate as the selling price.

How is bank fixed deposit interest compounded in India?
Most Indian commercial banks calculate and compound fixed deposit interest on a quarterly basis (every 3 months) for tenures of 6 months or longer. The mathematical formula is: A = P × [1 + (r ÷ 4)]^(4 × t).

How does temperature conversion work between Celsius and Fahrenheit?
Temperature scales have different zero points and degree sizes. To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit: °F = (°C × 9 ÷ 5) + 32. To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = (°F - 32) × 5 ÷ 9. Absolute temperature in Kelvin is K = °C + 273.15.
Are US gallons and UK Imperial gallons identical?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Mean, Median, and Mode?
The Mean is the arithmetic average (sum divided by count). The Median is the exact middle value when numbers are sorted in order. The Mode is the number that appears most frequently in the dataset.
When should I use the Median instead of the Mean?
The median is preferred when a dataset contains extreme outliers or is heavily skewed (such as household income, real estate prices, or net worth), because a few extreme numbers can distort the mean while leaving the median unaffected.
What is the difference between Sample and Population Standard Deviation?
Sample standard deviation (s) divides the squared deviations by (n - 1) (Bessel's correction) to provide an unbiased estimate when working with a subset sample of a larger group. Population standard deviation (σ) divides by n and is used when you have data for every single member of the population.
Can a dataset have more than one mode, or no mode?
Yes. If two values tie for highest frequency, the dataset is bimodal. If three or more tie, it is multimodal. If every value appears with equal frequency (such as all appearing once), the dataset has no mode.
Statistical Reference: Standard deviation and mean calculations assume numerical real values. For specialized datasets involving logarithmic growth, finance rates, or categorical data, consider evaluating geometric means or domain-specific weighting.
